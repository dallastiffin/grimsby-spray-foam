## The two products

| | Closed-cell | Open-cell |
|---|---|---|
| R-value per inch | R-6.0 to R-7.0, ~R-6.5 after thermal drift | R-3.5 to R-3.8 |
| Installed cost | $1.50–$3.50 / board ft | $0.80–$1.50 / board ft |
| Vapour barrier | Yes, at sufficient thickness | **No** |
| Air barrier | Yes | Yes |
| Structure | Rigid, adds racking strength | Soft, flexible |
| Best for | Crawl spaces, rim joists, basements, agricultural, anywhere moisture matters | Interior walls, sound damping, budget attic work in conditioned space |

The vapour barrier difference is the single most useful thing to explain to a
homeowner, and most competitor sites skip it.

## Code and certification — Ontario

This is the trust differentiator. Very few competitor sites explain it.

- Installers must be **trained and certified**. **CUFCA** (Canadian Urethane
  Foam Contractors Association) is the recognised body.
- Material must meet **CAN/ULC S705.1**; installation must meet **CAN/ULC
  S705.2**. Flame spread and smoke development per **CAN/ULC-S102.2**.
- **Thermal barrier:** exposed foam in occupied space must be covered. ½-inch
  gypsum drywall is the standard accepted assembly. Approved spray-applied
  intumescent coatings are an alternative under **OBC 9.29.4–9.29.9**.
- **Ignition barrier:** in spaces not normally occupied — attics, crawl spaces —
  a thinner tested ignition barrier is often acceptable instead of a full
  thermal barrier.

Homeowners routinely do not know foam has to be covered. A page explaining
thermal vs ignition barrier answers a real question and demonstrates competence.

## Rebates — Ontario, verify before quoting

**Home Renovation Savings (HRS)** is the current program, delivered by Save on
Energy (IESO) with Enbridge Gas, backed by the Government of Ontario.

- Replaced **Home Efficiency Rebate Plus (HER+)**, which closed February 2024.
  Do not reference HER+ as live.
- Standalone attic insulation rebate: **$800–$1,250**, increased by $250 in
  January 2026. Some 2026 sources quote "up to $1,000" for this stream.
- **No energy assessment required for the standalone attic rebate.**
- Attic must reach **R-50 or higher** to qualify. Note this now matches the
  2024 OBC new-build ceiling figure exactly.
- Open to Enbridge Gas residential customers who own their home.
- Program extended to **30 November 2026**.

### Multi-measure stream — different rules, and this is where copy goes wrong

Sources reviewed for the Grimsby build report a larger **multi-measure**
insulation stream (figures around **$7,700**, and caps quoted as up to **$5,000**
for gas-heated and up to **$10,000** for electrically heated homes) which **does**
require a pre-work and post-work energy assessment.

**The distinction matters and the two streams get conflated constantly:**

| | Standalone attic | Multi-measure |
|---|---|---|
| Energy assessment | **Not required** | **Required, before work starts** |
| Amount | ~$800–$1,250 | Larger, varies by heating type |

An early draft of this site's copy said an assessment is needed in all cases.
That was wrong and was corrected. If a homeowner only wants the attic done,
telling them to book an audit first costs them money and time for nothing.

Figures vary between sources. The site names **no amount at all**, says the
amount depends on heating type and scope, distinguishes the two streams, and
states plainly that the business is not the program administrator. Keep it that
way — a wrong rebate figure on a live site is worse than no figure.

**This date needs a diary reminder.** Any page citing it should be reviewed
before **30 November 2026** or it becomes wrong.


## Pricing — market ranges, NOT this business's prices

**Flag this to the owner every time.** Board-foot figures in the table at the
top of this file are the more precise basis; the per-square-foot ranges below
are what 2026 consumer-facing Ontario sources quote, and are what the FAQ uses
because that is the unit homeowners think in.

- **Open cell:** roughly **$1.50–$2.50 per sq ft**
- **Closed cell:** roughly **$3–$7 per sq ft**

One source quoted a 1,000 sq ft attic at $4,500–7,000 open cell / $6,500–10,000
closed cell. Those do **not** reconcile with the per-square-foot ranges above
unless significant thickness is assumed, so **the site does not use them**.

The FAQ gives the per-square-foot ranges, says plainly they are market ranges
rather than a quote, and stresses that **access drives the number more than
material does** — a walk-in basement and an eighteen-inch crawl space can take
similar material and cost very differently.

The old SitePanda site claimed "$3 to $7 per square foot" for garage insulation
specifically. Within the closed-cell range, but not carried over as a claim.

## Failure modes — high-intent search territory

Strong content angle and it justifies certification directly.

- **Off-ratio foam.** The two components mixed out of balance. Causes poor
  adhesion, shrinkage and cracking.
- **Persistent odour.** A lasting chemical smell indicates off-ratio
  installation. Uncured foam can off-gas VOCs and isocyanates for weeks or
  months, occasionally longer.
- **Shrinkage / pulling away.** Foam retreating from rafters or studs, sometimes
  half an inch or more, opens continuous air channels and destroys the air
  sealing that was the point of the job.
- **Spraying too cold.** Substrate below the product's minimum causes the same
  shrinkage and adhesion failures.
- **Visual tells:** crumbly or brittle texture, discolouration, delamination
  from framing or sheathing, cracking within days or weeks.

Remedy is mechanical removal back to clean substrate, then reinstallation. It
cannot be fixed by spraying over it.

## OBC SB-12, Climate Zone 1 — updated for the 2024 code

Grimsby is **Zone 1** (under 5000 heating degree days below 18°C — Southern and
Eastern Ontario). The **2024 OBC has been in force since 1 January 2025** and
moved the numbers up. New construction, heating equipment at AFUE ≥ 90%:

| Component | 2024 code | Previously |
|---|---|---|
| Ceiling with attic | **R-50** | R-40 |
| Ceiling without attic | **R-31** | R-28 |
| Walls above grade | **R-24** | R-19 |
| Basement walls | **R-20** | R-12 |

Basement walls nearly doubled. That is the headline worth leading with.

The 2024 update also puts far more weight on **airtightness testing and
whole-building energy modelling** than nominal R-value alone, which is the
strongest available argument for foam on a new build.

Figures vary by compliance package and heating equipment — always defer to the
building department.

## Grimsby and west Niagara specifics

Replace this section wholesale for a different city.

- **The escarpment splits the town — this is the best angle here.** Grimsby runs
  from the Lake Ontario shore up to the face of the Niagara Escarpment, rising
  **100–200 m above lake level** inside the town boundary. Communities at the
  base and top of the escarpment get **20–40% more snowfall** than flat lakeshore
  ground 20–30 km away, through orographic lift — air forced up the escarpment
  face cools, hits dew point sooner and drops more. Nobody else in the trade
  writes about this. It justifies genuinely different advice for the upper and
  lower halves of one small town.
- **Grimsby hovers around zero rather than staying frozen.** January runs roughly
  **-7.3°C to -2.3°C** by source; the region rarely drops below about -18°C, with
  more frost-free days and lighter snow than most of the province. The angle is
  that a mild climate is not an easy one — repeated crossings of 0°C mean
  cavities warm, hold moisture, then chill, and every crossing is a condensation
  opportunity. This is why the copy emphasises air sealing over raw R-value.
  It is the opposite emphasis to a northern city and different again from
  Windsor's summer-humidity argument.
- **Grimsby Beach.** Painted Victorian "gingerbread" cottages, originally an
  1880s Methodist camp meeting ground, some **over 150 years old**, many now
  lived in year-round. Balloon framing, stone or rubble foundations, crawl
  spaces rather than basements. Completely different retrofit advice, and a
  strong local-authenticity signal.
- **Casablanca / Grimsby-on-the-Lake.** Master-planned waterfront condos and
  townhomes built out over roughly a decade off the back of the GO station and
  the Toronto commute. This is the new-construction market. **Nelles Estates**
  is the mature-trees, larger-lot older detached area.
- **Population 31,864 (2026 estimate)**, one of the fastest-growing
  municipalities in Niagara — 7.9% growth 2011–2016. **11,395 occupied
  dwellings, 64.4% single-detached, 82.7% owner-occupied.**
- **Municipal geography — get this right or a local spots it.** Beamsville,
  Vineland, Jordan and Campden are **neighbourhoods of the Town of Lincoln**,
  not separate towns. Smithville and Caistor Centre are West Lincoln. Winona and
  Stoney Creek are Hamilton. The site says the Lincoln part explicitly.
- **Real local features used in copy:** Main Street West/East, Ontario Street,
  Casablanca Boulevard, Winston Road, Mountain Road, Ridge Road, Nelles Road,
  Elm Street, Beamer Memorial Conservation Area, Forty Mile Creek, the Bruce
  Trail, the QEW.
- **Do not reuse Windsor's framing.** Windsor is flat, hot, humid, on clay, with
  a basement-flooding history and an automotive economy. Grimsby is topographic,
  lake-moderated and residential. Different mechanism, different lead argument.

## Copy cautions

- Do not state rebate amounts without the program name and the review date.
- Do not claim a specific R-value for a finished assembly without saying the
  thickness it assumes.
- Do not imply foam can be left exposed. It cannot, in occupied space.
- Pricing is a market range, not this business's quoted price. Confirm with the
  owner before publishing figures.
